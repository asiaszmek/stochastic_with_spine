Learning and plasticity is governed by calcium dynamics, which is tightly controlled in neurons by e.g. buffering by proteins such as calmodulin. Calmodulin itself controls dynamics of plasma membrane Ca2+ ATPase (PMCA) and ryanodine receptors (RyR), which are activated by calcium and release it from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). In CA1 pyramidal neurons, RyR2 (inhibited by calmodulin) and RyR2 (activated by calmodulin) are found in the dendritic shafts and spines, respectively. Because of calmodulin abundance in the brain, RyRs are constitutively bound to it. However, CaM oxidation, observed in aging and neurodegeneration, lowers its affinity for its targets, changing calcium homeostasis.

We wanted to confirm that spine ER helps transduce Ca transients in healthy neurons and investigate the effect of higher oxidation levels accompanying old age on spatial and temporal specificity of Ca transients.

We developed a multi-compartment stochastic reaction-diffusion model of signaling pathways underlying calcium regulation in a 51 um long apical dendrite of a CA1 pyramidal neuron with a spine with and without ER. For the old age condition of old age, we also simulated over-expression of RyRs, L-type calcium channels, increased cytoplasmic calcium buffers and lowered cam affinity for its targets.
